Start with the money you are already losing

Most Nigerian businesses do not have an AI problem. They have a leak problem. An Instagram enquiry sits unanswered overnight. A WhatsApp customer asks for a price, waits two hours and buys from someone else. A skilled staff member spends the morning answering the same ten questions. An invoice goes unpaid because nobody had time to follow up.

Each of these leaks is small on its own. Together they are often the difference between a business that grows and one that stays stuck. My years in real estate sales taught me how often the first credible response wins the conversation. AI agents are, first of all, a way to plug these leaks at a cost that a growing business can afford.

The profit is rarely in the AI. It is in the work the AI stops you from losing.

Five ways AI agents add profit

1. Capture leads that would otherwise go unanswered

Customers message at 11pm, on Sunday afternoons and on public holidays. An AI agent replies instantly at any hour, answers the first questions and books the next step, so the lead is still warm when your team arrives in the morning.

2. Reply faster and convert more

Speed sells, especially when customers are messaging several businesses at once. When an agent responds in seconds with accurate prices, availability and next steps, fewer buyers drift to competitors. If you spend money on ads, faster replies also mean more return from every naira of ad spend.

3. Give your team hours back

Repetitive questions consume the time of people who should be selling, serving key customers or running operations. When an agent handles the routine questions, the same team can handle more customers without immediate new hires.

4. Stop leaks in orders, payments and collections

Agents can confirm orders, send payment links through platforms such as Paystack or Flutterwave, remind customers before due dates and give delivery updates. Small improvements in follow-up often bring cash in faster and reduce abandoned orders.

5. Turn conversations into business intelligence

Every customer question is data. An agent can group what people ask about, what they complain about and why they do not buy. That tells you which prices, products, policies or pages need to change, insight that usually disappears inside chat history.

What this looks like by industry

  • Retail and e-commerce: answer price, size and availability questions on WhatsApp and Instagram, take orders and share payment links.
  • Real estate: respond to property enquiries from ads, qualify budget and location, and book inspections for the sales team.
  • Clinics and pharmacies: book and confirm appointments, send reminders and answer opening hours and service questions, leaving clinical advice to professionals.
  • Schools and training providers: answer admissions, fees and schedule questions during busy enrolment periods.
  • Logistics: give tracking updates, collect delivery details and escalate failed deliveries.
  • Hospitality and shortlets: handle availability, bookings, directions and check-in questions.

How to estimate the return before you build

You do not need a complex model. Take one workflow and estimate three numbers: extra revenue from leads you would otherwise miss, staff time saved and leakage reduced. Here is an illustrative example. Replace every number with your own.

A business receives 600 WhatsApp enquiries a month. About 150 of them arrive after hours or wait more than an hour for a reply. If an agent turns just 5% of those 150 into sales, that is 7 extra sales. At ₦25,000 gross profit per sale, that is about ₦175,000 a month. If the agent also handles 60% of routine questions at four minutes each, the team gets back about 24 hours a month.

Compare that with the monthly running cost and the build cost spread over a year. If the numbers only work under heroic assumptions, choose a different workflow. If they work with conservative assumptions, you have a strong case to build.

The costs to plan for

  • Build: design, development, integrations and testing. Scope grows with every system the agent must connect to.
  • AI model usage: usually billed per use in US dollars, so it moves with the exchange rate.
  • Channel fees: the WhatsApp Business Platform charges for certain message types.
  • Improvement: updating knowledge, reviewing conversations and fixing gaps every month.
  • Your team's time: someone needs to own the workflow and approve what the agent says.

When an AI agent will not pay off

An honest AI engineer will tell you when not to build. An agent is the wrong investment if you receive only a handful of enquiries a week, if your process changes daily and nobody can describe it, if your information is scattered and out of date, or if customers are leaving because of the product itself rather than the service. Fix those first. AI amplifies a working process. It rarely rescues a broken one.

Agents should also not make high-stakes decisions on their own, such as approving credit or rejecting applicants. Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, people have protections around significant decisions based solely on automated processing, and keeping a person accountable is good business anyway.

A 30-day way to start

  1. Week 1: choose one workflow, collect real conversations and record your baseline numbers.
  2. Week 2: build a prototype agent on your real questions, prices and policies.
  3. Week 3: pilot it after hours or on a share of traffic, with your team reviewing replies.
  4. Week 4: compare results with the baseline and decide whether to expand, adjust or stop.
