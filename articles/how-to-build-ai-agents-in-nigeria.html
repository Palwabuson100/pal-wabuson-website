What an AI agent actually is

An AI agent is software that can understand a request in plain language, decide what to do next and take action using the tools it has been given. It might answer a customer on WhatsApp, look up an order, create a payment link, book an appointment or alert a member of staff.

That is the difference between an agent and the old generation of chatbots. A scripted chatbot follows a decision tree and breaks when a customer phrases something unexpectedly. An agent reasons over the conversation, draws on your real information and can complete multi-step work.

Every useful agent has the same building blocks:

  • A model, the large language model that reads, reasons and writes.
  • Instructions that define its role, tone, limits and when to escalate.
  • Knowledge from your approved documents, price lists and policies.
  • Tools that let it act: your CRM, order system, calendar or payment platform.
  • Memory of the conversation and, where appropriate, the customer's history.
  • Guardrails that keep it accurate, safe and honest about what it cannot do.
  • A channel where people actually talk to it.

Step 1: Choose one job worth automating

The most common mistake I see is trying to build an agent that "handles everything". Start with one job. The best first jobs share four traits: they happen often, they are repetitive, success can be measured and a mistake is cheap to correct.

Strong first projects for Nigerian businesses include answering product and price enquiries on WhatsApp, qualifying property or service leads from ads, booking appointments for clinics and salons, giving order and delivery updates, and answering admissions or fees questions for schools.

Poor first projects are those where the agent would make a final, high-stakes decision on its own, such as approving credit, rejecting a job applicant or giving medical advice. Agents can support those processes, but people should own the outcome.

Step 2: Map the workflow as it works today

Before writing a line of code, study the work. Gather a sample of real conversations, with personal details removed, and list the questions that come up most often. You will usually find that a small set of questions makes up most of the volume.

Then answer four questions. Where does the information to answer each question live? Who handles the exceptions today? What does a good outcome look like? And what number will tell you the agent is working: response time, enquiries answered, appointments booked, hours saved or sales closed?

Write the baseline down. Without it, you will not be able to prove that the agent paid for itself.

Step 3: Meet customers on the channel they already use

In Nigeria, that is usually WhatsApp. Customers already message businesses there, so an agent on WhatsApp removes friction instead of asking people to download an app or fill a form.

For a production agent, use the official WhatsApp Business Platform rather than unofficial tools that automate the WhatsApp Business app, which risk getting your number banned. You can connect through Meta's Cloud API directly or through a business solution provider. Plan for a verified Meta Business account, approved message templates for messages your business starts outside the customer service window, and Meta's per-message charges.

WhatsApp is not the only option. Instagram direct messages matter for fashion, beauty and food brands. Web chat suits B2B buyers. Voice agents can serve customers who prefer to call, and SMS still reaches people on basic phones.

Step 4: Choose the model and the build approach

Most business agents today run on hosted models from providers such as Anthropic, OpenAI and Google. They offer the strongest reasoning and are quick to start with, but they are billed per use, in US dollars. Open-source models can be self-hosted for privacy or cost reasons, but they need infrastructure and expertise to run well.

You also need to choose how to build. No-code and low-code platforms such as n8n, Make and Voiceflow are good for simple agents and fast experiments. Custom code gives you full control over integrations, data handling, testing and cost, which matters once an agent touches payments, customer records or high volumes.

Whatever you choose, keep the model swappable. Prices and capabilities change quickly, and you should be able to move to a better or cheaper model without rebuilding the whole agent.

Step 5: Give the agent knowledge and tools

An agent is only as good as the information it can reach. Give it a curated knowledge base of approved documents, such as price lists, delivery zones, policies and FAQs, and set it up to retrieve the relevant parts for each question. Assign someone to keep that information current. Out-of-date prices are one of the fastest ways to lose customer trust.

Then connect tools so the agent can act: check order status, create a Paystack or Flutterwave payment link, book a slot in a calendar, add a lead to your CRM or Google Sheet, or notify a staff member. Give each tool the least access it needs, and require human confirmation for anything that moves money or changes important records.

Never let an agent confirm a payment because a customer sent a screenshot. Confirm payments only through your payment provider or bank, using a verified webhook or reference check.

Step 6: Design for Nigerian realities

Language. Customers write in Nigerian English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and mixtures of all of them, often in the same message. Modern models handle much of this, but quality varies. Test with real messages from your own customers, and make sure the agent replies in a tone that fits your brand.

Cost and exchange rates. Because most AI services are priced in dollars, a weaker naira raises your running costs. Control spend by using smaller models for simple steps, keeping prompts and context lean, caching common answers and setting usage alerts. Make sure you have a reliable way to pay for foreign services, since some naira cards have had international spending limits.

Power and connectivity. Host the agent in the cloud, not on a computer in your office, so it keeps working through outages. Design replies for customers on slow or expensive data: short messages, light images and no heavy downloads.

Trust. Many Nigerian customers are cautious about scams. Use your verified business number, tell customers they are speaking with an AI assistant and make it easy to reach a person.

Step 7: Build guardrails and human handoff

Good agents are built to fail safely. Instruct the agent to answer only from approved information and to say when it does not know. Define clear triggers for handing over to a person: complaints, refunds, angry or distressed customers, legal or medical questions and anything outside its scope. When it hands over, it should pass a short summary so the customer does not have to repeat themselves.

Data protection is not optional. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 sets out how personal data must be handled, including a lawful basis for processing, collecting only what you need, keeping it secure and respecting the rights of data subjects. The Act also gives people protections around decisions based solely on automated processing that significantly affect them, which is another reason to keep a person responsible for consequential decisions.

Log every conversation, restrict who can see the logs and set a retention period.

Step 8: Test, launch small and measure

Build a test set from real questions, including awkward ones: misspellings, Pidgin, angry messages, attempts to trick the agent and questions it should refuse. Run the agent against this set every time you change it.

Then launch in a controlled way. A good pattern is to let the agent handle conversations outside working hours first, or a share of daytime traffic, while your team reviews its replies. Track response time, the share of conversations resolved without handoff, conversions or bookings, customer satisfaction and cost per conversation. Review the numbers weekly and improve from real mistakes.

Build it yourself or hire an AI engineer?

If you need a simple FAQ assistant and are comfortable with no-code tools, you can build a first version yourself using the steps above. Once the agent needs to connect to payments, customer records or several systems, handle high volume or meet data protection obligations, an experienced AI engineer will save you time, money and risk.
